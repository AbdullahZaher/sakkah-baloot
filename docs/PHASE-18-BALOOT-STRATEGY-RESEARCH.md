# Phase 18 — Baloot Strategy Research\n\n**Status:** Adopted as AI heuristics, not rules  \n**Phase:** 18 — Competitive AI & Single-Player Match\n\n## Purpose\n\nPlayer strategy research is incorporated into the AI architecture as probabilistic decision evidence. It must never override the authoritative game engine.\n\n## Strategy findings adopted\n\n### 1. Card counting\n\nThe AI should maintain a card-memory layer for:\n- own hand\n- exposed card\n- completed/current trick cards\n- declared projects and Baloot\n- inferred remaining cards\n\nHigh-impact ranks such as trump J/9 and side-suit A/10 should receive explicit tracking because their disappearance materially changes future trick control.\n\n### 2. Partner-aware trick evaluation\n\nWinning a trick is not equivalent to maximizing team value. The AI should recognize cases where the partner already controls the trick and avoid unnecessary intervention, including unnecessary trump expenditure.\n\n### 3. Trump extraction\n\nDrawing opposing trump can be valuable when it improves future side-suit control, but it is not a fixed rule. The evaluator must compare:\n- estimated opposing trump distribution\n- partner benefit\n- future entries\n- side-suit winners\n- remaining control cards\n\n### 4. Minimum winning card\n\nWhen multiple legal cards win the same trick, the AI should evaluate the smallest sufficient winner rather than automatically selecting the strongest card. Preserving future control is a first-class heuristic.\n\n### 5. Information value\n\nEarly tricks can reveal suit distribution and voids. The evaluator should score information gained from a play, not only immediate points.\n\n### 6. Belief-based partner/opponent reading\n\nObserved play patterns may update beliefs about hidden cards. These are probabilistic signals, not deterministic conventions. The model must retain uncertainty.\n\n### 7. Kaboot planning\n\nKaboot evaluation should consider the complete remaining round:\n- trick control\n- trump control\n- entries\n- opponent voids\n- partner support\n- remaining high cards\n- last-trick control\n\nKaboot is therefore a planning objective, not a simple hand-strength threshold.\n\n### 8. Last-trick value\n\nThe final trick's bonus means the AI should account for expected final-trick control in round evaluation rather than treating all tricks as equal.\n\n### 9. Projects and Baloot\n\nProjects and Baloot are tactical components of total expected round value. Their values must be evaluated together with trick-taking and contract success, while legality and scoring remain engine-owned.\n\n## Evidence handling\n\nStrategy articles and player guides are treated differently from the official rules.\n\n- **Rules:** authoritative engine behavior.\n- **Strategy:** heuristic evidence.\n- **Player conventions:** probabilistic signals only.\n- **Experimental ideas:** must be validated by simulation before becoming production policy.\n\nResearch references used during this planning pass include:\n- https://balootx.com/\n- https://www.elblot.com/ar/learn/official-rules/\n- https://8k.sa/\n\nThe research does not authorize any rules change.\n\n## AI architecture impact\n\nThe following components are now explicit Phase 18 targets:\n\n1. CardMemory\n2. BeliefState\n3. StrategyFeatureExtractor\n4. HeuristicPolicy\n5. ImmutableSimulationAdapter\n6. InformationSetSampler\n7. IS-MCTS\n8. EndgameSolver\n\nThe implementation order remains observation boundary → authoritative action space → deterministic RNG → baseline heuristic policy → simulation → belief sampling → IS-MCTS.
